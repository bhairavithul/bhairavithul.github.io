Tinker Village

Stabilizing a platform through systems thinking, design systems & sprint management — bringing an information architecture to a growing app that had been shipping without one.

Duration
04 months
Role
UI/UX Design Lead

AProblem

What was broken for the team?

No information architecture existed for the team to build against, so the app was breaking with every release. Without a shared source of truth for how screens, states, and features fit together, engineering and design were effectively re-negotiating the product's structure on every sprint.

BMy Role

What was my contribution?

As UI/UX Design Lead, I was brought in to stabilize the platform through systems thinking rather than incremental screen fixes — building the information architecture, design system, and sprint-level process the team needed to stop shipping regressions.

CKey Insights

What actually shaped the direction?

Without documented architecture, every release was a gamble

Engineering had no shared source of truth for how the app was structured, which is a direct line to why APK builds had ballooned to roughly 30 a month.

Most breakage was preventable before a line of code was written

Once use cases were mapped upfront, the vast majority could be caught at the spec stage instead of in production.

Reward systems needed their own dedicated logic layer

Bolting gamification onto an undocumented structure was part of what made releases fragile in the first place.

A consistent design language was infrastructure, not decoration

It's what let design and engineering stay aligned as the sprint cadence tightened.

DDesign Decisions

How did each insight turn into a call?

Insight → Without documented architecture, every release was a gamble

Decision: Built the foundational information architecture and logic specifications before any further screens were built — giving engineering an actual structure to build against instead of reverse-engineering intent from whatever shipped last.

Insight → Reward systems needed their own dedicated logic layer

Decision: Designed the reward and gamification system as its own documented module with defined rules and states, rather than a set of one-off screens layered on top of the core product.

Insight → A consistent design language was infrastructure, not decoration

Decision: Established a documented design system so components, spacing, and patterns stayed consistent as more of the team touched the product — the same kind of single source of truth the architecture work gave to structure, applied to visual and interaction language.

FValidation & Collaboration

How was it tested against reality?

A dedicated bug-tracking and stress-testing process, run in Notion, gave the team a shared, visible way to catch issues before they shipped — closing the loop the missing architecture had left open.
