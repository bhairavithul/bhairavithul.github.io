DG Lock

DG Lock is an ambitious project in Saudi Arabia designed to create a sustainable and technologically advanced city. Within it, a digital locker provides residents with a secure and convenient solution for managing and storing their personal digital assets, enhancing both security and accessibility.

AProblem

What was broken for stakeholders?

In DG Lock, residents need a secure and user-friendly digital locker to manage and store their digital assets. Current solutions often fall short in security, usability, and integration. The challenge was to create a digital locker that provides robust security and seamless access within DG Lock's advanced technological ecosystem.

Primary Research

Initially, the approach was a little confusing. Using the snowballing technique, a survey (via Google Forms) was circulated among friends and colleagues to get a variety of information. To reduce language barriers, only English speakers were surveyed.

User Interviews

Out of 54 participants, 32 people were ready for the interview. Each 45-minute conversation covered four focus areas:

🙋

Personal Experience: How people currently manage and struggle with digital assets like documents, photos, and passwords.

🛡️

Security Concerns: How much security mattered to them, and any past experience with a breach.

♿

Convenience & Accessibility: How often and from which devices they access their digital assets.

👍

Trust & Perception: What would build their trust, including how they felt about a government-backed solution.

Sample size: 32 participants

DKey Insights

What actually shaped the direction?

Security was the top trust lever, not just a feature ask

Participants tied their trust in a digital locker directly to how secure it felt — more than to convenience or how polished it looked.

Convenience across devices mattered almost as much as security

Digital assets get used throughout the day, not stored and forgotten, so participants expected access from whichever device was on hand.

Government-backing cut both ways on trust

Asking how people felt about a government-backed locker surfaced real hesitation — trust had to be earned through the experience itself, not assumed from who built it.

Regional growth meant designing for more than one language, from day one

The influencing-factors research (tourism, social influence, technology adoption) pointed to a broader regional audience than English-only, well before language came up as its own question.

EDesign Decisions

How did each insight turn into a call?

Insight → Government-backing cut both ways on trust

Decision: Framed DG Lock as a single digital-government entity built around minimal physical interaction and strict confidentiality — so trust was designed into the product's role as a medium between citizens and government, not left to the government's reputation alone.

To build a digital government platform that acts as a single entity catering to individuals and businesses inside DG Lock, having minimum physical interaction, maintaining the confidentiality of the data, and being the medium between the citizens and the government.

Turning research into real people, not assumptions

Decision: Built personas and empathy maps directly from the survey and interview data, then derived user journey maps from them — so the direction reflected two real usage patterns instead of a single generic user.

User personas were created using details from both primary and secondary research. The personas helped empathize with and understand what users might say, think, do, and feel. After inferring the empathy map, the user journey map was deduced.

Insight → Convenience across devices mattered almost as much as security

Decision: Mapped user flows directly from the journey maps before any screens were drawn, so the path to a document stayed short and consistent regardless of which device someone picked up.

Using the inference from the user journey map, the user flows were created.

Aligning direction before going high-fidelity

Decision: Built lo-fi mockups first and walked the PO team through them, so the approach was validated before investing time in polished screens.

Using a digital method, lo-fi mockups were created to showcase the ideas. This not only gave a clear picture but helped the PO team understand the approach.

Insight → Security was the top trust lever

Decision: Every document uploaded to the app got password protection or a time bound by default, rather than as an opt-in setting — since security was the most consistent condition for trust across interviews.

Secure Files / Folders

Security was the biggest factor that led to making every document uploaded on the app have password protection or a time bound. This not only increased trust but gave privacy too.

Insight → Regional growth meant designing for more than one language

Decision: Built RTL layouts for Arabic alongside the LTR designs from the start, rather than as a later localization pass, so the product was ready for a wider regional rollout.

There is a high possibility that in future the app may have more language privileges. In that case, RTL language design layouts are also ready.
